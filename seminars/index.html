Liam Gameng | 12 February | Q-Learning with Mean-Semideviation Markov Risk Measures

Speaker: Liam Gameng

Title: Q-Learning with Mean-Semideviation Markov Risk Measures

Date/Time: 12 February, 2027, 10:00-11:00 AM

Abstract: We consider Q-Learning in finite-horizon Markov decision processes in which the quality of a policy is evaluated by a time-consistent Markov risk measure constructed from the first-order mean-semideviation transition risk measure. Exploiting its structure within the multipattern risk averse approximation framework, we propose a risk-averse least-squares value iteration (LSVI) method which performs two coupled ridge regressions per episodes: one approximates the conditional mean of the risk-averse value function and the other the mean-semideviation risk using the mean estimation as its anchor. Earlier risk-averse LSVI methods involved mini batches of N>1 conditionally independent draws from the transition kernel, which requires a generative model or simulator. However, this method is fully online with data requiring only a single realized transition of the trajectory. We prove a high-probability regret bound of order $\bar{\mathcal{O}}\big((1+\kappa)^Hd^{3/2}H^2\sqrt{K}\big)$, where $\kappa\in[0,1]$ is the risk aversion level, $d$ the feature dimensions, $H$ the horizon, and $K$ the number of episodes. The empirical performance of the algorithm for different levels of risk-aversion is demonstrated using a deep Q-learning adaptation of the method on a robot collection problem.

Speaker Bio: Liam Gameng is a second year Ph.D. student at the Management Science and Information Systems department at Rutgers Business School, and is advised by Professor Andrzej Ruszczynski. His research interests are in risk-averse dynamic programming and deep reinforcement learning, with applications in financial and defense settings. He holds a bachelors in Applied Mathematics and Statistics as well as a masters in Quantitative Finance from Stony Brook University.

Geneviève Gauthier | 12 March | Deep Hedging with Option-Market Information

Speaker: Geneviève Gauthier

Title: Deep Hedging with Option-Market Information

Date/Time: 12 March, 2027, 10:00-11:00 AM

Abstract: Dynamic hedging of option portfolios requires a state vector that captures information from both the underlying asset and the option market. Incorporating the full implied volatility surface raises two challenges: its cross-section must be summarized by a parsimonious set of state variables, and the resulting dynamic optimization problem becomes too high-dimensional for conventional Bellman recursions. We address these challenges using implied volatility factor models and reinforcement learning. For S&P 500 index options, five economically interpretable factors summarize the implied volatility surface and enter a joint model for index returns and surface dynamics. This model provides the market simulator used to train a recurrent deep-hedging strategy with the index and an option as hedging instruments, while explicitly accounting for transaction costs. Simulation experiments and a historical out-of-sample backtest compare the resulting strategies with conventional delta and delta-gamma hedging and assess the contribution of implied volatility information to risk reduction. The final part of the presentation considers individual-stock options. A deep implied volatility factor model incorporates time to earnings announcements while retaining a parsimonious daily representation of the surface, with applications to risk-neutral density estimation and stock-specific volatility indices.

In 2018, she received the Statistical Society of Canada Award for Impact of Applied and Collaborative Work for her outstanding contributions to the promotion of innovative statistical methodologies in financial engineering. Her research focuses on financial engineering, mathematical finance, and financial econometrics, with applications to derivative pricing, risk management, volatility modelling, credit risk, and financial and energy markets. Koray D. Simsek | 30 April | ETFs and the Declining Index Inclusion Effect: Implications for Optimal Index Tracking

Speaker: Koray D. Simsek

Title: ETFs and the Declining Index Inclusion Effect: Implications for Optimal Index Tracking

Date/Time: 30 April, 2027, 10:00-11:00 AM

Abstract: Joint work with Greg J. Mecca and Brian R. Walkup. Considerable growth in passive investing and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) has raised questions about their influence on market efficiency. Index additions and deletions provide a setting for examining the price effects of demand and supply shocks associated with benchmark changes. Extending established event-study methods to S&P 500 changes going back to 1993, we investigate the relationship between ETF growth and the index inclusion effect. Consistent with prior research, we find significant abnormal returns around index changes over the full sample. However, their magnitude declines substantially over time, with deletion effects approaching zero in the most recent decade and smaller positive effects remaining for additions. Annual and quarterly regressions reveal a statistically significant association between ETF growth and the declining magnitude of abnormal returns over the longer sample periods. We also document a decline in tracking errors among S&P 500 index funds over the same period. These findings have implications for optimal index tracking, particularly the tradeoff between tracking risk and the costs of rebalancing around benchmark changes.

Joonhyuk Suh | 11 September | Loss-Versus-Rebalancing for Multi-Asset Geometric-Mean Market Makers

Speaker: Joonhyuk Suh

Title: Loss-Versus-Rebalancing for Multi-Asset Geometric-Mean Market Makers

Date/Time: 11 September, 2026, 10:00-11:00 AM

Abstract: Many decentralized exchanges (DEXs) use automated market makers (AMMs), in which trades are executed against liquidity pools according to deterministic reserve-based rules. When external asset prices move, arbitrage trades realign the pool with the external market, creating an adverse-selection cost for liquidity providers that can be measured by loss-versus-rebalancing (LVR). We study LVR in Balancer-style multi-asset automated market makers with a weighted geometric-mean invariant. We derive the arbitrage-aligned reserves and pool value and obtain a closed-form LVR identity: instantaneous LVR equals one half of the pool value multiplied by a weighted sum of pairwise relative-return quadratic variations. The formula shows that multi-asset LVR depends on both individual asset variances and cross-asset covariances. We then construct an exact pathwise benchmark and covariance-based LVR predictors and illustrate them using cryptocurrency price data.

Speaker Bio: Joonhyuk Suh is a Ph.D. student in the Department of Applied Mathematics and Statistics at Stony Brook University. His research interests include convex optimization, machine learning, algorithmic fairness, and quantitative methods for cryptocurrency trading. Alongside his doctoral studies, Joon currently works as a Quantitative Research Intern at XBTO in New York.

Guilherme Larangeira | 25 September | Pricing State Support and Offtake Risk in a Critical-Minerals Supply Chain: A Contingent-Claims and Newsvendor Approach

Speaker: Guilherme Larangeira

Title: Pricing State Support and Offtake Risk in a Critical-Minerals Supply Chain: A Contingent-Claims and Newsvendor Approach

Date/Time: 25 September, 2026, 10:00-11:00 AM

Abstract: Brazil holds the world's second-largest rare-earth reserves but currently exports raw concentrate rather than finished magnets, forfeiting most of the value in the mine-to-magnet chain. This talk presents two linked pricing problems from an ongoing dissertation studying what it would take for a Brazilian project (Colossus, developed by Viridis Mining and Minerals) to close that gap. The first problem prices a state-backed price floor — a guarantee that a producer receives at least a minimum price per kilogram for its output — as a strip of European put options, using Black-76. The floor is calibrated against 26 years of USGS price data and validated against a peer project's own disclosed feasibility study. The second problem asks whether an offtake (take-or-pay) commitment, which protects the producer against a buyer's demand risk rather than price risk, can be priced the same way. It cannot: demand is not a tradeable underlying, so the no-arbitrage machinery behind Black-76 does not extend to it. The talk focuses on the redirection this forced — toward the risk-averse newsvendor and CVaR-based supply-chain-contract literature (Rockafellar & Uryasev, 2000; Zhou et al., 2014; Jammernegg, Kischka & Silbermayr, 2024) — and the open question of adapting a wholesale-price-contract coordination result to a take-or-pay-specific contract structure.

Kaiming Cheng | 23 October | Moment density estimator with scaled Hermite polynomials

Speaker: Kaiming Cheng

Title: Moment density estimator with scaled Hermite polynomials

Date/Time: 23 October, 2026, 10:00-11:00 AM

Abstract: We address the problem of approximating an unknown probability density function from a finite set of its moments. Instead of attempting to recover the true underlying distribution, we provide a continuous density designed to closely interpolate the given moments. We use an estimator constructed from scaled Hermite polynomials as the baseline and demonstrate its equivalence to the bias-corrected Kernel Density Estimator (KDE). By viewing the scale parameter as bandwidth, this connection allows us to derive a principled bandwidth selection rule based on minimizing the Mean Integrated Squared Error (MISE). To resolve the common drawbacks of existing Hermite-series-based methods that estimators may lack proper normalization or produce negative values, we formulate a quadratic programming (QP) objective. By restricting the target space to weighted Hermite polynomials and optimizing within the Gram metric, we ensure valid, continuous density recovery for sampling while maintaining computational efficiency. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method, when utilizing high-order Hermite basis, consistently yields valid density functions that maintain high fidelity to the target moments with an implicit moment extension.

Speaker Bio: Kaiming Cheng is a Ph.D. student in the Applied Mathematics and Statistics department at Stony Brook University. His current research interests include moment problems and generative modeling.

Lichun He | 6 November | From Blackjack to Finance: Reinforcement Learning with Expanding Action Spaces

Speaker: Lichun He

Title: From Blackjack to Finance: Reinforcement Learning with Expanding Action Spaces

Date/Time: 6 November, 2026, 10:00-11:00 AM

Abstract: Reinforcement learning (RL) is often studied in settings where the available actions are fixed. In real decision problems, however, agents may gain access to additional actions, some of which occur much less often than others. More choices can create greater flexibility, but they can also make learning harder when the agent has limited opportunities to practice rare but important decisions. Our initial work studies this problem using Blackjack as a controlled RL environment. We compare increasingly complex action spaces, beginning with Hit and Stand and then adding Double and Split, across several RL algorithms, including Monte Carlo methods, SARSA, Q-learning, deep Q-networks, and proximal policy optimization. The results suggest that performance can decline as additional actions are introduced, and that a substantial portion of this loss is associated with errors involving the newly available actions. This ongoing work extends the same framework to financial decision-making problems, where agents may similarly face actions that are infrequent but consequential. The goal is to investigate how action availability, action frequency, and learning exposure interact to affect policy performance. The finance extension will provide an initial application of the framework beyond Blackjack and explore whether similar learning challenges arise in a financial setting.

Speaker: Yao Xie

Title: Convex Estimation for Nonlinear Dependent Data via Variational Inequalities

Date/Time: 17 April, 2026, 10:00-11:00 AM

Abstract: Classical generalized linear models provide a powerful framework for relating covariates to responses, but they are often too restrictive for dependent data such as nonlinear time series, point processes, and spatio-temporal observations. I will illustrate the framework with examples from nonlinear autoregression and spatio-temporal count modeling, including a real-data application to wildfire incidents. More broadly, the talk shows how monotone operator methods can provide a useful bridge between modern dependent-data modeling and tractable statistical inference.

Joon Suh | 1 May | Convex Optimization Algorithms for Fair Classification with POE Parity

Speaker: Joon Suh

Title: Convex Optimization Algorithms for Fair Classification with POE Parity

Date/Time: 1 May, 2026, 10:00-11:00 AM

Abstract: We study fair binary classification where a decision is made when the score exceeds some threshold. We considered the Probability-of-exceedance (POE) parity, i.e., the probability that the score exceeds a threshold is equal for sensitive groups. POE parity is a difficult nonconvex optimization constraint. We show that a simple convex program combining a standard convex loss (e.g., logistic regression) with convex constraints can achieve the exact POE parity. The modeling involved group- specific intercepts ensuring a monotone response of each group tail probability to its own intercept. The equality POE constraints are replaced with three types of convex constraints on: (1) buffered probability of exceedance (bPOE), (2) conditional value-at-risk (CVaR), (3) expectations. The optimization problems are quickly solved with convex solvers. We demonstrated the approach with four benchmark datasets: Adult Income, COMPAS, Default Credit, and Law School. The approach: (1) attains essentially zero empirical POE gap (perfect fairness at the target threshold), (2) achieves lower mean-squared error (compared to other published approaches), (3) uses simple constraints, and (4) is much faster than nonconvex POE formulations.

Dr. Jonathan Schachter | 12 September | Statistics of Financial Risk Measures

Speaker: Dr. Jonathan Schachter

Title: Statistics of Financial Risk Measures

Date/Time: 12 September, 2025, 10:00-11:00 AM

Abstract: Given observed departures of financial profit-loss distributions from Normality, mean and variance provide an incomplete quantitative description. Statisticians likely would prefer the incorporation of skew and kurtosis, eg Cornish Fisher. However, the industry prefers description by order statistics, the largest signed loss being the minimum. In practice, legacy Normal thinking in finance conditions a need for percentiles to describe the left tail alpha, variously 5%, 1% or 0.1%.
This talk begins with the challenges of using a point risk estimate (VaR, PFE etc.). For VaR, at least for general risk, historical simulation provides estimates. But these are generally reported without taking into account standard errors due to finite sampling. These can be large (but are not currently reported to senior management or regulators), so I provide the tools to do so — order statistic variances. As for PFE, Monte Carlo is required, but convergence in order statistic differs from convergence in mean (eg in a pricing model). Starting values for pseudorandom generation (seeds; used commonly for the inverse transform method) provide additional variance — again not reported currently. Here I also provide practical tools. Finally, I discuss quasirandom numbers, e.g. Sobol sequences, where scrambling replaces a user-selected seed.
This discussion is related to content in Schachter et al., “A First Course in Model Validation and Model Risk Management” (London: Elsevier, forthcoming January 2026). That is, two chapters on market risk, three on credit risk, one on miscellaneous risk, and a statistics refresher appendix.

Prof. Mikhail Smirnov | 26 September | Multivariate Kelly Criterion, and Calculation of Optimal Leverage

Speaker: Prof. Mikhail Smirnov

Title: Multivariate Kelly Criterion, and Calculation of Optimal Leverage

Date/Time: 26 September, 2025, 10:00-11:00 AM

Abstract: The Kelly criterion introduced in 1956 by John L.
